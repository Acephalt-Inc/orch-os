# Product Demo Storyboard

Create an executable storyboard for a short software-product demo. The viewer should understand the user problem, see one credible product action, and know how its result can be checked—without relying on narration or audio alone.

## Establish the evidence boundary

Before writing shots, separate verified product evidence from proposed visuals. A screen, capability, metric, workflow outcome, or user or client claim that is not verified must be marked **unverified placeholder**. Supplied material establishes a claim only when it is approved evidence for that claim; a supplied mock, planned screenshot, inferred workflow, or generated visual remains unverified.

Ask for the minimum missing inputs only when they change the claims: audience, available screens or recording, primary user question, and preferred runtime. If evidence is unavailable, still provide the storyboard with explicit capture placeholders.

## Write the storyboard

Choose a single viewer question for each shot. The sequence usually moves from the user’s input or problem, through one system action, to a visible result and the way a user can verify it. Give every shot:

- timestamp and duration;
- the one question it answers;
- on-screen content and evidence status;
- concise narration and matching caption;
- the continuity anchor and transition to the next shot.

Use real product captures where possible. Keep UI readable at viewing size; crop or zoom only to clarify the responsible action or state. Preserve information a professional user needs to judge the result, such as labels, source references, status, or review controls. Use a transition only when it carries continuity through the same object, workspace, cursor action, state change, or question.

Design for silent comprehension: a viewer who reads only the screens and captions should still identify the input, the system action, the outcome, and any stated limitation. Treat audio and sound as optional emphasis, never as the sole bearer of meaning.

After drafting, run this check:

1. Can each shot be summarized by one question without repeating another shot?
2. Does every product claim point to supplied evidence or carry an unverified-placeholder label?
3. Does each cut have a stated continuity reason, or should it be a direct cut?
4. Can a muted viewer explain what changed and how it is checked?

Keep motion and visual style appropriate to the actual brand and medium. Do not apply another company’s visual identity or imply its endorsement.
