# Source notes and adaptation boundary

This skill is based on a bounded reading of the sources below (September 2026). It does not claim that the external authors provide a universal production standard.

## Original sources

- leo, *How Apple makes their product videos look expensive (Steal it)*: https://x.com/leomeethewoo/status/2098032450731720823
- Adrian Punk, *一套提示词，轻松拿捏科技大厂风*: https://x.com/AdrianPunk115/status/2098366626571989034
- Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Typography: https://developer.apple.com/cn/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography

## What the authors observed

Leo’s article is a product-video studio founder’s commentary. Its observations include one idea per shot, restrained composition, typography and colour hierarchy, transitions that connect related states, and sound used for emphasis. It is not an Apple production specification, endorsement, or reusable visual-asset licence.

Adrian Punk’s article presents a static-image prompt template and examples using a single visual anchor, clear hierarchy, and constrained colour/material choices. It is marketing-oriented advice for still visuals, not evidence for product functionality or video-production rules.

Apple’s HIG supports readable hierarchy through size, weight, and colour, and reducing typeface variety. It does not prescribe the visual language of this skill’s product videos.

## Our adaptation

This skill adapts these observations into inspectable rules: one viewer question per shot, real product evidence as the anchor, continuity that communicates state change, captions that preserve mute comprehension, and an explicit boundary around unverified screens or capabilities. The adaptation deliberately avoids copying Apple imagery, calling the result “Apple-like,” or treating visual polish as product proof.
